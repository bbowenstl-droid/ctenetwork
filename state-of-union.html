CTE: State of the Union

Every Move Has a History

A league-wide investigation by CTE Bot • November 13, 2026

Completed games through 2026 Week 9. Transactions and rosters captured November 13 at approximately 11:20 a.m. Central. The announced three-team agreement remains a separate pending event.

The newest trade in CTE involves three owners. Its backstory involves considerably more.

Brendan is preparing to acquire Javonte Williams. Mike is preparing to receive Antonio Williams and Jacob’s 2027 first. Dan is preparing to receive Adonai Mitchell. Taken alone, it is a contender buying help, a struggling team buying time, and a third owner making a receiver swap.

But Antonio Williams entered the league on a second-round pick that originally belonged to Brendan. Brendan sent that pick to Dan in October 2025, in the same trade that brought Kyren Williams and Rico Dowdle the other way. Almost a year later, Dowdle returned to Dan in the package that brought Brendan Bhayshul Tuten and Jacob’s first. Now Antonio and that first are scheduled to converge on Mike’s roster.

Two branches of one old trade are meeting again.

Javonte’s story reaches back further: Jacob sent him to Mike in CTE’s first recorded completed trade. Mitchell’s route includes waivers, drops, and reacquisitions. This is not a clean chain of one player becoming another. It is a league building its own history, one decision at a time.

That is the state of CTE. There are only two seasons in the archive, but the consequences already overlap.

The beginning: a draft, two replacements, and an upset champion

The startup draft began September 1, 2025, at approximately 8:30 p.m. Central. Twelve franchises made 252 selections across 21 rounds. The league began with recognizable foundations: Ja’Marr Chase at the head of Jeremy’s roster, Justin Jefferson on Brett’s, and the collection of assets that each owner would either protect or dismantle.

There are two ownership changes that matter to any honest account. The site’s historical ownership records place Jeremy in charge of the franchise now run by Isaiah for Weeks 1–3 of 2025; Isaiah’s tenure begins in Week 4. Carter took over Tyler’s franchise for 2026. Their inherited picks and players belong in franchise histories. Their predecessors’ wins and decisions do not automatically become personal accomplishments.

The inaugural regular season belonged to Jerry and Jacob. Jerry finished 25–3 in the combined standings, split between a 12–2 matchup record and a 13–1 median record. Jacob went 24–4, with identical 12–2 records against opponents and the median. Their scoring totals were 2,122.64 and 2,054.96.

Brendan finished 17–11, with a 9–5 matchup record and 8–6 against the median. He had scored 334.90 fewer regular-season points than Jerry. He then beat Brett in the opening playoff round, Jerry in the semifinal, and Jacob in the championship, 141.12–115.48.

The first CTE title therefore established two facts that still frame the league: Jerry and Jacob could sustain elite regular seasons, and Brendan could beat them when the trophy was on the line. Neither fact erases the other.

The 2025 deal that keeps producing new branches

On October 28, 2025, Brendan and Dan completed the trade that now sits near the center of the league’s transaction map.

Brendan received: Kyren Williams, Rico Dowdle, and Dan’s 2026 first.

Dan received: Cam Skattebo, Tre Tucker, and Brendan’s 2026 second.

The picks stopped being abstract the following August. Dan’s first became Jordyn Tyson at 1.06, selected by Brendan. Brendan’s second became Antonio Williams at 2.12, selected by Dan. Tyson remains on Brendan’s current roster; Antonio remains on Dan’s at the reporting cutoff.

Dowdle made the return trip on October 10, 2026. Brendan sent him and Matthew Golden to Dan for Tuten and Jacob’s 2027 first. Golden adds a waiver branch: Elijah dropped him August 2, Brendan acquired him August 4, and he became part of that October trade package.

Kyren has supplied 159.10 points in Brendan’s submitted starting lineups through nine completed weeks this season, the most by any player for that team over that period. That is a measurable contribution from the old trade. It does not prove how many wins the trade created, because that would require knowing which replacement players Brendan would otherwise have started.

Now the proposed three-team agreement would send Antonio and Jacob’s first to Mike while delivering Javonte to Brendan and Mitchell to Dan. It is the kind of connection that disappears if trade coverage begins and ends with the latest notification.

There is no honest way to say “Skattebo became all of this.” The original deal contained several assets on both sides. There is, however, a documented path from that transaction to two pieces of the latest agreement. That is more interesting than an inflated trade-calculator victory lap.

Jacob’s first has an itinerary

Jacob’s 2027 first began its travels on November 5, 2025, when he acquired Garrett Wilson from Dan. Dan received Harold Fannin Jr., Rashid Shaheed, and that first.

Shaheed had arrived on Jacob’s roster only eight days earlier in a trade with Cotton. Cotton received Keenan Allen and Jacob’s 2026 and 2027 fourths. The 2026 fourth later became Drew Allar at 4.11.

The first stayed with Dan until the Tuten trade in October 2026, then moved to Brendan. Its proposed next stop is Mike. At the cutoff, that third transfer has not completed.

Garrett Wilson took a different branch. In April, Jacob traded him and his 2028 fourth to Carter for Drake London. Carter then used that fourth, alongside his own 2026 third and 2027 second, to acquire Patrick Mahomes from Mike. The 2026 third became Michael Trigg at 3.05.

London stayed with Jacob until November 12. Brendan acquired him for Tyler Warren, Brendan’s 2028 third, and Brendan’s 2029 second.

One 2025 Garrett Wilson transaction now has documented connections to London, Tuten, Mahomes, Warren, and the pending three-team agreement. They are connected transactions, not a claim that Wilson alone paid for every subsequent return.

The two-slot draft move that reached November

On August 1, before the rookie draft, Carter sent Rhamondre Stevenson and his original first to Mike for Mike’s original first. The confirmed draft board makes the exchange concrete: Carter moved from 1.05 to 1.03. He selected Jadarian Price. Mike selected Carnell Tate at 1.05.

Price did not remain with Carter. On November 4, he joined Bo Nix and Parker Washington in a package going to Jacob, along with Carter’s 2029 second. Carter received DeVonta Smith, Tyler Shough, Isaiah Likely, Devaughn Vele, and Jacob’s 2029 third.

That means the August decision to spend Stevenson to move up two slots did not simply produce a rookie Carter would keep. It produced one of the assets in his next major roster reconstruction. Whether that sequence proves worthwhile depends on the combined outcomes, not just which rookie looks better on an isolated ranking today.

Parker Washington: the round trip

Washington may be the best compact example of why the ledger needs more than trades.

Brendan picked him up in November 2025 and dropped him in December. Jacob acquired him in August 2026. That waiver pickup is a new acquisition; Brendan cannot be credited with Jacob’s later trade proceeds.

From there, Washington made three completed trade stops: Jacob to Mike on August 27; Mike to Carter on September 23; Carter back to Jacob on November 4.

The August trade also sent DeVonta Smith from Mike to Jacob. The November deal sent Smith from Jacob to Carter while Washington returned to Jacob. Their routes crossed twice, inside different packages with different picks and players attached.

Washington is the most frequently traded player in this completed ledger, with three transfers. His journey is a better illustration of CTE’s market than a simple “winner” stamp: managers repeatedly exchanged the same asset to pursue different roster combinations.

Two contenders, two very different construction methods

Jacob has participated in 10 of CTE’s 21 completed trades. Jerry has participated in one.

Both stand at 16–2 through nine completed weeks of 2026. Both are 7–2 against their scheduled opponents. Both are 9–0 against the median.

Jacob has the scoring advantage: 1,472.60 points to Jerry’s 1,333.58, a gap of 139.02. His all-play record is 85–14, meaning his weekly score would have beaten 85 of the 99 other team-scores available across those weeks. Jerry’s is 78–21.

Jacob’s lineup has absorbed a series of major trades: Chase Brown, CeeDee Lamb, Henderson, London before his departure, and more recently Price and Warren. Five of his 21 startup selections are on his current roster, including the Packers defense. The original core has survived selectively, while the surrounding roster has been repeatedly replaced.

Jerry’s nine startup selections still present include Brock Bowers, De’Von Achane, Jaxon Smith-Njigba, George Pickens, Rashee Rice, Caleb Williams, and Drake Maye. His lone completed trade sent Daniel Jones to Brendan for two picks. That deal did not build his offensive core.

The waiver wire did add something significant. Jerry acquired Christian Watson on October 26, 2025. Watson has delivered 176.90 points in Jerry’s submitted lineups through Week 9 this season, second on that team to Smith-Njigba’s 232.06. Those figures measure points actually started for Jerry, not total NFL production or a projection.

The lesson is not that constant trading works or that patience works. Both have produced contenders here. Neither guarantees the championship that Brendan already owns.

What the standings conceal

The combined standings reward both the weekly matchup and the median. Reading them without the split can hide how teams reached the same neighborhood.

Isaiah is 14–4, built on an outstanding 8–1 matchup record and a 6–3 median record. His 59–40 all-play record remains comfortably positive, but its 59.6% win rate is well below his 88.9% head-to-head rate. He has also faced the lowest cumulative opponent scoring this season: 1,061.24 points.

That does not invalidate his wins. It explains why his matchup record is stronger than his rank in cumulative scoring, where he sits behind Jacob, Jerry, Troy, and Brendan.

Dan illustrates the other side. He is 2–7 in matchups, 4–5 against the median, and 38–61 in all-play comparisons. His record has been worse than his scoring would suggest against a randomly selected opponent, although the scoring itself has still been below league average. His 138.08–138.78 loss to Isaiah in Week 4 is the season’s closest completed game.

Mike’s 1–17 cannot be blamed entirely on the schedule. He is 1–8 head-to-head and 0–9 against the median. His all-play record, 22–77, confirms that this has been a difficult scoring season as well as a difficult results season. The 1,234.32 points scored against him are the most faced by any team, so the schedule has also offered little relief.

Troy’s inaugural season supplies the clearest example of why the median matters: he went 6–8 against opponents but 11–3 against the median. Those additional wins helped produce a 17–11 combined record and a playoff appearance. The standings credited a strong scoring team that the head-to-head record alone undersold.

All-play is a descriptive comparison, not a simulation of alternate lineups, matchups, or owner decisions. Its value is in showing how often a team’s actual score beat the rest of the league that week.

The games the league should remember

The closest: Jacob 142.56, Troy 142.38, in the very first week of 2025. The margin was 0.18. CTE began producing grievances immediately.

The early upset: Elijah beat Jacob 141.52–140.92 in 2025 Week 3. That 0.60-point result matters when recounting Jacob’s inaugural season: he was excellent, but he was not undefeated.

The biggest blowout: Jacob beat Jesse 188.60–64.40 in 2026 Week 5, a 124.20-point margin.

The largest combined score: Jacob’s 205.92–163.62 victory over Brendan in 2026 Week 4 produced 369.54 points. Brendan scored more than enough to win many games and still lost by 42.30.

The highest team score in the competitive archive: Jerry’s 211.66 against Elijah in 2026 Week 5.

The championship: Brendan 141.12, Jacob 115.48, in 2025 Week 17. A 25.64-point margin decided the first trophy.

These records cover regular-season games and championship-path elimination games. Placement and consolation results remain available in the explorer but are excluded from these competitive superlatives.

Rivalries: history before hype

Brendan leads Jacob 2–1 across their regular-season and title-path meetings. Brendan won their 2025 regular-season meeting and the championship; Jacob answered in this year’s 369.54-point shootout. It is the league’s most consequential three-game argument.

Elijah and Isaiah are 1–1 in their actual owner-versus-owner regular-season meetings. Elijah won in 2025; Isaiah answered in Week 7 this year. Their subsequent six-player trade moved Lamar Jackson, Amon-Ra St. Brown, and Woody Marks to Isaiah, with Chase, DK Metcalf, and RJ Harvey going to Elijah. Isaiah also received Elijah’s 2027 third. Their next chapter will be played with pieces that used to belong to each other.

Jerry leads Elijah 2–0 in regular-season meetings, and also won their 2025 third-place game. The distinction matters: the placement result adds to their broader history, but it was not a championship-elimination win.

Jesse–Troy, Dan–Mike, and Brett–Cotton are each 1–1 in regular-season meetings. Jacob has won his only completed meeting with Carter. Their rivalry has considerably more transaction history than matchup history so far.

What is left to spend

The future-pick ledger helps explain the next phase. Mike currently owns six selections in 2027 and eight in 2028. That 2028 inventory includes his own first and Jacob’s first. The pending three-team deal would add Jacob’s 2027 first, bringing Mike’s 2027 total to seven if it processes unchanged.

Jacob currently holds one 2027 pick: his own second. His original first is with Brendan, his third with Mike, and his fourth with Cotton. That is a concrete cost of the transactions that helped reshape his roster. Recent deals have replenished later years, including two acquired 2029 seconds and Brendan’s 2028 third.

Brendan currently has three picks in each of 2027, 2028, and 2029. His 2027 inventory consists of his own first, Jacob’s first, and his own fourth. Sending Jacob’s first in the pending deal would leave two 2027 selections.

Isaiah and Cotton each hold six 2027 picks. Jesse has all four of his own selections in each of the three future draft years covered by this inventory. Those are different forms of flexibility, and they should not be reduced to raw totals alone: a first and a fourth are not interchangeable.

No future selection is assigned an exact draft slot. The league has not finished the season that will help determine those outcomes.

The state of the union

CTE’s first champion is buying again. Its two strongest regular-season franchises remain at the top, using very different methods. Isaiah has turned an inherited franchise into the league’s best current head-to-head record. Carter has changed almost everything around his inherited roster. Mike has accumulated future picks while suffering through the present. Several quieter owners still have the cores they drafted and the freedom to choose what comes next.

The most revealing fact is not who has traded the most. It is how often today’s roster contains yesterday’s decision in a different form.

A fourth became Drew Allar. A first became Price and then entered a seven-player trade. A waiver pickup became part of the Tuten package. A second became Antonio Williams and is now waiting to meet a first-round pick that traveled through a different branch of the same old deal.

The league is young enough that most of its history can still be traced. It is already old enough that nobody should trust their memory to do it.

Reporting notes

This feature uses fresh Sleeper records, Flaim league tools, and the CTE repository’s documented ownership periods and published pending-trade report. Completed trade IDs were reconciled for every requested week; game scores were cross-checked between Flaim and Sleeper; calculated head-to-head plus median records were reconciled with reported standings for both seasons. Draft positions and original pick owners come from the confirmed draft results.

The current Flaim standings response labels the season as playoffs in progress and marks some teams as having made the playoffs. Sleeper’s league settings instead show Week 10, nine scored weeks, and a Week 15 playoff start. The current bracket has no completed results. This feature therefore does not treat those bracket placeholders as clinched berths or playoff games.

Trade dates use completion timestamps converted to America/Chicago, which can differ from the day an agreement was announced. A pending trade cannot be included in the completed transaction totals. The three-team agreement is sourced to the site’s November 13 report; its player transfers and pick ownership remain hypothetical until completion.

“Startup selections still present” means membership on both the draft list and today’s roster. It does not imply uninterrupted ownership; Dowdle, for example, left Dan and returned. Multi-asset trade packages are not allocated artificial one-for-one returns. Drops break direct trade lineage, even if the player is later reacquired.

This is a retrospective based on league production, not a current NFL injury report, dynasty price guide, or causal estimate of wins created by a trade. Unknown owner motivations are not presented as quotes or facts. Future-pick totals cover 2027–2029 under the current four-round settings. The remaining raw data, calculation scripts, franchise profiles, full trade ledger, and audit are included with this feature.
